With Liquid Glass + on, the bar stays out of every screen recording, so in this film Crest's own code draws it over a recording of the desktop.

A taskbar of its own: Crest can take the place of the Windows taskbar with a bar in its style, live previews of your windows, widgets from the tools you have in the notch and Liquid Glass +. Also a Material style for the whole app, the settings in a window of their own, focus mode back and what you asked for after 1.2.

**New**

- Crest's taskbar (Settings → Taskbar → General → **Crest taskbar**, off by default, Windows only, not in the Microsoft Store version for now): it hides the Windows taskbar and shows Crest's on every monitor, with Windows' Start, your pinned apps and open windows, widgets, the Notification area and the Crest button. Turn it off, or quit Crest, and the Windows taskbar comes back. Settings → Taskbar → Appearance sets the Shape: Docked runs along the whole bottom edge and keeps maximized windows above it; Floating, the default, stands a little off the edges; Capsule is as wide as its buttons; Tiles puts each button on a tile of its own. Height, Icon size, Background and Transparency are there too, and colours and theme come from Settings → Themes.
- Live previews: hold the pointer on an app with open windows and a live preview of each shows above its button, as in Windows 11. A click on a preview brings that window forward and its cross closes it. Open the previews with a click and the arrow keys, Enter and Esc work too. The live pictures never show in screenshots or screen shares.
- Glass for the bar (Settings → Taskbar → Appearance → Glass: Off, the default, Glass or **Liquid Glass +**; Windows 11 24H2 or later on x64, not in the Microsoft Store version for now). Both show a live copy of the screen behind the bar, renewed up to 10 times a second, so a window moving under the bar shows through it as it moves. Glass bends the rim and blurs like the notch's glass. Liquid Glass +, adapted from [LiquidGlassWindows](https://github.com/pxlkeshi/LiquidGlassWindows) (MIT), draws a strong lens along the edge, red and blue a little apart, almost no blur, with white icons and text, and darkens by itself over a light screen so the icons and the clock stay readable. While either is chosen the bar is left out of every screenshot, recording and screen share, and Crest uses more battery; the setting says so.
- Widgets from your tools: Weather, the Clock, Hardware and the Timer come in versions you pick in Settings → Taskbar → Widgets, each with a preview of how it looks on the bar, and **Add tool** puts a button for one of Crest's tools on the bar. Widgets, tool buttons and Status drag along the bar to a new place, in their zone or another; Esc or a drop outside the bar changes nothing.
- The Crest button opens a menu with Task Manager, Add widget and Auto-hide.
- The Notification area sits on the bar like a widget: the tray icons you keep visible get a button each, the others wait behind ^, and an icon dragged from there onto the bar stays.
- Status shows the network, the volume and the battery as one button. A click opens a quick panel with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth tiles, the volume and its output, the brightness, Energy saver and Airplane mode. The Wi-Fi page lists the networks nearby and connects to them, the Bluetooth page lists the paired devices with their battery.
- The Material style (Settings → Themes → Style → Material, Windows and Mac; Crest stays the default): every Crest window in Material 3, with colours worked out from your theme, text in Google Sans Flex, Material Symbols icons and Material's springs. In the settings window, contour lines in the theme's colours drift behind the pages, after [Stellaria](https://github.com/SMIDR76/stellaria-windows); **Animated background**, under Style, turns them off. Resting the pointer on a theme's card previews it on the window, and Weather and Hardware take the layout of Stellaria's dashboard. Details in [themes.md](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/blob/main/docs/themes.md#material-style).
- The settings open in a window of their own, from the gear, a right click on the notch, Settings … in the tray menu or by starting Crest again. It shows in Alt + Tab, follows the theme, and every change shows on the notch at once.
- Focus mode is back ([#55](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/55)). Turn on **Focus mode with three clicks** in Settings → Display (off by default): three quick clicks on the closed notch make it half transparent or a line and let clicks through to the app below. Three more clicks, the shortcut or a restart bring it back.

**New in the tools you have**

- Each AI quota ring shows the weekly limit outside and the 5-hour session in a thinner ring inside, so the session no longer hides behind the weekly number ([#57](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/57)).
- Status alerts can show everywhere: with Settings → Display → Status alerts → **Show everywhere** on (off by default, Windows), volume, brightness, the lock keys, the microphone and Copy also show over full-screen apps and while Crest is hidden, without taking the focus. With Glass on they are clear glass (Windows 11 24H2 or later on x64, not in the Microsoft Store version for now); the Status alerts switch under Settings → Themes → Glass, on by default, turns that off.
- New status alerts: Copy (off by default) says what kind of thing you copied, never the text itself. The others show the output device, Do not disturb and focus sessions, Energy saver and power mode, Airplane mode, monitors connected or disconnected with their refresh rate, a full battery on the charger and the microphone muted from anywhere. Changes made in Crest itself do not show.

**Fixes**

- With Windows' Text size above 100%, the open panel no longer closes when the pointer reaches its lower controls, and the **Keep open** pin takes clicks again: every window of the notch now follows the Text size ([#61](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/61)).
- The detached timer grows with Windows' Text size and stays on its monitor ([#61](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/61)).
- The notch no longer stays open when you switch tools while typing on WebKit, which is mostly the Mac ([#58](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/pull/58)).
- A picture copied twice shows once in the clipboard history, as text already did ([#59](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/pull/59)).
- With **Hide the Windows indicator** on, Windows' own volume slider stays hidden even when another app registers a taskbar window.
