Crest's taskbar now does much of what Windows' own does: jump lists, the badges and progress apps report, Windows' app menu and buttons, the Win+number keys, a place on the top edge and the combine setting. Everything for the taskbar is on Windows only and, like the taskbar itself, not in the Microsoft Store version for now. In the notch, the tool bar can move under the header and lose its background, each part of Overview gets a switch, and Note and Calc change their text size.

**New**

- Jump lists: a right click on an app shows its jump list above the menu, as on Windows 11, with the app's own entries, its Recent or Frequent files and its Tasks, each opening with that app. Recent and Frequent follow Windows' **Show recently opened items in Start, Jump Lists, and File Explorer**. Tasks and an app's own categories come from an undocumented Windows file and are left out when it cannot be read. Items pinned in Windows' jump list do not show, and apps with no Start entry or app id of their own have no jump list.
- An app's right-click menu on the bar has Windows' rows: the app's name to start a new instance, Pin to taskbar or Unpin from taskbar, Open file location for an open app, and Close window or Close all windows. End task, which ends the app at once without saving, joins them with Settings → Taskbar → Apps and windows → **End task in the app menu** (off by default); it spares the explorer.exe that draws the desktop, and an app running as administrator may refuse it. For a window that is not responding, the rows act on the hung app behind it. Shift+click or a middle click starts a new instance, and Ctrl+Shift+click starts one as administrator after Windows asks, except for apps from the Microsoft Store.
- Any app with an open window can be pinned from its right-click menu, a portable exe or a development build too. An app that asks Windows never to be pinned has no **Pin to taskbar** row. A pin added or taken off on Crest's bar does not reach Windows' taskbar.
- An exe, a shortcut or an internet shortcut (a Steam game's .url, say) dragged from File Explorer onto the bar's apps is pinned where the drop line shows ([#74](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/74)).
- Pinned apps drag along the bar to a new order. Letting go off the bar or pressing Esc leaves them as they were.
- Badges and progress that apps put on Windows' taskbar buttons show on Crest's bar too: unread counts in Telegram, Discord or Outlook, or a download's progress line. Crest receives them the way Windows' taskbar does, which Microsoft does not document, and through its own notification area, so they show only while a bar holds the **Notification area** and no other program stands in for Windows' tray. A badge's text is not read from apps in a low-integrity sandbox, and the buttons a player puts under its preview are not on Crest's previews yet. Windows an app keeps off Windows' taskbar stay off Crest's bar too.
- Settings → Taskbar → General → **Windows keys on the bar** (off by default): Win+1 to Win+9 and Win+0, alone or with Shift, Ctrl, Ctrl+Shift or Alt, and Win+T and Win+B act on Crest's bar as on Windows' taskbar ([#71](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/71)). Explorer restarts when the switch or the bar is turned on or off and after Crest quits, which may close open File Explorer windows. It works through an undocumented Explorer setting: while Crest runs as administrator it never restarts Explorer and the keys stay with Windows, the setting says when Windows keeps the keys or another program holds some of them, and uninstalling Crest gives them back to Windows from the next sign-in.
- Inside the bar, the arrow keys, Home and End move between buttons, Shift+F10 or the menu key opens a button's menu, and Esc hands the keyboard back. A screen reader hears each app's state: pinned, how many windows are open, in front or asking for attention.
- Settings → Taskbar → Appearance → **Position** puts the bar on the bottom edge (the default) or the top edge of every monitor. On top, menus, previews and panels open downward and a notch on that edge moves to the bottom; Start, search and Windows' own panels still open at the edge of Windows' taskbar ([#73](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/73)).
- Settings → Taskbar → Apps and windows → **Combine taskbar buttons and hide labels** works like Windows' option: Always, the default, keeps one button per app without a label, Never gives each window a button with its title, and When taskbar is full does that while the titles fit.
- Ctrl+click brings an app's windows forward one after another. Settings → Taskbar → Apps and windows → **Middle click** set to Close closes an app's windows instead of starting a new instance (New instance is the default); an app with unsaved work asks first, and one running as administrator may refuse. Show the number of windows, off by default, puts a small count on apps with two windows or more.
- Settings → Taskbar → Items → **Add Windows button** puts Windows' own Search, Task view, Windows widgets and Show desktop buttons on the bar. Windows widgets shows on the main bar only.
- **Input language**, another Windows button, shows the keyboard language of the window in front ("ITA" over "IT") and opens Windows' switcher on a click. It shows on the main bar, and only while more than one keyboard layout is installed.
- **Privacy indicators**, another Windows button, shows a microphone, camera or location icon while an app uses them and opens Windows' privacy page on a click. It shows on the main bar only and leaves out Crest's own use.
- **Recycle Bin**, another Windows button, shows whether the bin holds anything and opens it in File Explorer. Its right-click menu has Open and Empty Recycle Bin, which asks for Windows' own confirmation first. It shows on the main bar only.
- A **Disk space** widget (Settings → Taskbar → Widgets) shows the free space of the system drive, the default, of every drive or of the fullest one. Its panel lists every drive, and a click opens one in File Explorer. It is read every five minutes and when the panel opens, has no part in the notch, and leaves out drives locked by BitLocker, virtual drives and drives under 1 GB.
- In the Disk space panel each drive has a round **Scan now** button. It lists the ten largest folders in the drive's root with their sizes, then More folders, Files and an estimated System and unreadable row; a folder opens in File Explorer or scans one level deeper. While it reads you see the progress and Stop, then Rescan. A scan stops after 3 minutes or 5 million entries, and its results stay in memory for 10 minutes, never on disk.
- A click on the main bar's clock, or Enter or Space, opens one panel with Crest's Notices on top and its Calendar below, each with its own title and buttons, whether or not those tools are switched on. With **Live notices** off the top half says so and offers Turn on, and on a screen too short for both the clock opens the Calendar alone. While Live notices is on, a badge on the clock counts the notices in that list. The clocks on the other monitors' bars still open Windows' notification centre.
- The quick panel behind Status has a **Volume mixer** page, opened beside the volume slider, with Windows' volume and each app's.
- The quick panel's buttons sit three to a row and add a Microphone mute switch, where a microphone is found, and Cast, Night light, Do not disturb and Accessibility. The last three open Windows' Settings.
- The mouse wheel over Status changes Windows' volume. With Settings → Taskbar → General → **Mouse wheel changes the volume** on (off by default), the wheel over an app changes that app's own volume and shows the level above its button, and anywhere else on the bar Windows' volume.
- Settings → Taskbar → General → **Show on all displays** (on by default) decides whether the other monitors get a bar; turned off, they have no taskbar at all. With it on, Show apps on picks where an app's button shows: All taskbars, Main taskbar and taskbar where the window is open, or Taskbar where the window is open, the default, and Clock on every taskbar, off by default, puts the clock on every bar.
- Settings → Taskbar → Apps and windows → **Show windows from all desktops** (off by default) lists the windows of every virtual desktop, and a click on one from another desktop switches there.
- Shift+right-click on an app opens a window menu like Windows' own (Restore, Move, Resize, Minimize, Maximize, Close) for a single window, or for several the group menu that cascades, stacks, tiles side by side, restores, minimizes or closes them all. Entries an app adds to its own window menu do not show.
- Settings → Taskbar → Appearance → **Open app marker** draws a dot (the default), a large dot or a line under an open app, a longer one for the app in front. In the Material style the app in front keeps its pill.
- Settings → Taskbar → Appearance → **Start icon** gives the Start button one of six icons: Windows (the default), Crest, Dots, Rocket, Sparkle or Star, drawn in the bar's colour at the size of the Windows mark. The Items list shows the one you chose.
- While you drag a widget, a tool button, a Windows button or Status along the bar, a red disc with a bin shows above the bar over the item's place (below it when the bar is on top). Rest on it until it swells and turns a deeper red with a ring, let go, and the item leaves the bar as with **Remove** in Settings → Taskbar → Items. Letting go anywhere else, on the disc before it is ready, or pressing Esc changes nothing. There is no undo: Add widget, Add tool and Add Windows button put the item back.
- Settings → Taskbar → General → **Double click on empty space** can show the desktop or open Task Manager; it does nothing by default.
- Turning off Settings → Taskbar → General → **Show flashing on apps** (on by default) stops apps that ask for attention from pulsing and lets them fold behind the … button like any other app.
- A file dragged from File Explorer and held over an app brings its window forward after a moment, or opens the previews of an app with several windows.
- Dragging a window's preview sideways changes the order of that app's windows on the bar until Crest restarts.
- Resting the pointer on a tray icon, on the bar or behind ^, now tells its app, as Windows' tray does, so apps that draw their own popup on hover show it.

**New in the tools you have**

- Settings → Window → Open panel → **Tool bar** set to Top puts the open notch's tool bar under the header instead of at the bottom (Bottom, the default). The panel keeps its size and the header still drags the notch ([#76](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/76)).
- **Tool bar background**, beside it and on by default, can take away the capsule behind the tool bar's icons: the current tool keeps only its mark, and the highlight shows only under the pointer or the keyboard focus. With Glass on there is then no glass capsule and no lens ([#76](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/76)).
- Each part of Overview (Clock, Weather, Activity, Focus, Keep awake, Volume, Brightness and Quick controls) has a switch of its own under Settings → Tools → To-dos and Overview → **Overview**, all on by default. At its default size Overview gets shorter by the rows you hide, while a size of its own (Compact, Spacious or a drag of its edges) stays as it is. A hidden part reads nothing (a hidden weather makes no weather request for Overview), and switching Keep awake off there also ends keep awake. One of the parts that always show has to stay on. On a Mac there are no switches for Focus, Keep awake and Volume, since Overview has no tiles there ([#76](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/76)).
- In the open notch a tool can be dragged off the tool bar onto the same red disc, which shows above the bar, or below it when the tool bar sits under the header. Once the disc is ready, letting go switches the tool off, as its switch in Settings → **Tools** does, and that switch brings it back. The last tool on the bar cannot be dragged (Windows and Mac).
- A **Frames per second** slider, from 10 to 30 in steps of 5 and 10 by default, sets how often the live glass behind the closed notch, the capsule and Crest's taskbar is renewed, and the live glass hints now give the chosen rate. It is one setting, under Settings → Themes → Glass once Glass is on and under Settings → Taskbar → Appearance once the bar's Glass is on, and it says that a higher value uses more battery. It needs Windows 11 24H2 or later on x64 and is not in the Microsoft Store version for now.
- With **Liquid Glass +** chosen for the taskbar (Settings → Taskbar → Appearance → **Glass**) and the notch's Glass on, the open notch's tool bar becomes a Liquid Glass + capsule too, over a live copy of the screen beneath it, renewed at the chosen frames per second. It needs **Tool bar background** on. While it runs, the open panel is left out of screenshots and recordings. It needs Windows 11 24H2 or later on x64 and is not in the Microsoft Store version for now.
- With no calendar connected, the Calendar shows the month beside the No calendar connected prompt and its **Add calendar** button, instead of the prompt alone with no date (Windows and Mac).
- When the CPU gives no temperature for 10 seconds, Hardware shows - °C on its row instead of leaving the reading out, and pointing at it says where Crest can read one. The Material style and screen readers get it too (Windows, [#67](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/67)).
- Hardware also reads the CPU temperature and package power from **Core Temp** while it runs, as a third source after HWiNFO and LibreHardwareMonitor, and names it as the source. Readings from a Core Temp set to both Fahrenheit and Distance to TjMax are not used (Windows, [#67](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/67)).
- In Note and Calc, Ctrl + and Ctrl - change the text size from 80% to 200% and Ctrl 0 goes back to 100% (Cmd on a Mac), wherever the focus is in the open tool. Each tool keeps its own size and Calc's rows grow with the text; Settings → Window → **Scale** still scales all of Crest ([#68](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/68)).
- With nothing playing, Now Playing says what to check when a player does not show. On Windows: Crest shows what Windows' media controls show (Win+A; on Windows 10, a volume key), and Spotify appears there only with the desktop overlay for media keys turned on in its settings. On a Mac: Crest needs permission to control Spotify or Music under Privacy & Security → **Automation**.
- The Launcher's offer under the tiles to use Alt+1 to Alt+6 has a × that hides it for good; the switch stays under Settings → Tools → Launcher → **Alt+1 to Alt+6** (Windows, [#70](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/70)).
- The Launcher can add apps with no Start entry, such as a portable exe or a game's .lnk or .url, with **Add from file** under + in its app list or by dropping the file on the open Launcher. The app becomes the same pin Crest's bar keeps, and other files dropped there still go to the Shelf (Windows, [#70](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/70), [#75](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/75)).

**Fixes**

- Icons in the hidden icons panel (**Show hidden icons**, the ^ on the bar) can be dragged into a new order inside the panel, and an icon dragged from the bar onto a spot in the panel lands there ([#65](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/65)).
- A right click on an icon in the hidden icons panel keeps the panel open while the app's own menu shows; the panel closes when that menu closes or another program comes to the front ([#65](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/65)).
- Apps pinned on Windows' taskbar without a Start entry, such as Steam or Epic games, sites pinned from Edge and portable exes, now show on Crest's bar and come along with Settings → Taskbar → Apps and windows → **Import from Windows**, started from their shortcut ([#74](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/74)).
- Now Playing can no longer be made shorter than its default height of 232 px, by dragging its edges or with the Compact size, so the title, the progress bar and the controls always fit. A shorter height saved before comes back up; the width still shrinks to 80% ([#64](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/64)).
- The mouse wheel over the strips of the Shelf, Capture's library and AI Usage scrolls them sideways, as it already did in the Clipboard, instead of switching to the next tool. When a strip has nothing hidden, the Clipboard included, the wheel switches tools as before with Settings → Window → Mouse → **Mouse wheel switches tools** ([#66](https://github.com/andrealo20/crest/issues/66)).
- AI Usage reads and writes much less on disk. It saves its log state at most every 10 minutes and on quit instead of after every refresh, flushes it to disk before it replaces the old file so a power cut cannot break it, drops records older than its 31 days and, on Windows, opens only the session logs that changed.
- The offline lyrics translator (Settings → Display → Music → **Translate offline**) stays loaded for 10 minutes after the last translation instead of 30 seconds, so each new song no longer loads the engine and its models from disk again. A song in another language starts a fresh engine with only its own models.

Try every tool in your browser before installing: [crestisland.app](https://crestisland.app/).
